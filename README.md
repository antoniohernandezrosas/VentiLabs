# VentiLabs
VentiLabs is an intelligent automated ventilation system based on artificial intelligence and AIoT technologies, designed to enhance safety, energy efficiency, and sustainability in enclosed environments such as laboratories, industrial warehouses, research centers, and urban spaces exposed to hazardous gas accumulation.

Air pollution is one of the most significant global health risks today. According to international organizations, millions of premature deaths each year are linked to exposure to toxic gases such as carbon monoxide, volatile organic compounds, and flammable gases. This issue is not limited to highly polluted megacities but also affects enclosed spaces where poor ventilation can lead to severe accidents, explosions, or silent poisons. However, most existing ventilation systems operate in a static, reactive, or manual manner, lacking intelligent adaptability and predictive analysis.

VentiLabs addresses this challenge through an AIoT architecture that integrates gas sensors (MQ-3, MQ-4, MQ-7, MQ-8, and MQ-135), environmental sensors for temperature and humidity, and an intelligent controller based on ESP32. The system continuously collects real-time data on pollutants such as carbon monoxide, methane, hydrogen, alcohols, and volatile organic compounds, and dynamically adjusts ventilation speed via PWM control. A minimum baseline ventilation is maintained, while visual and audible emergency protocols are triggered when dangerous concentration levels are detected.

The core innovation of VentiLabs lies in its hybrid algorithmic approach, which combines real-time intelligent control with predictive artificial intelligence models, surpassing traditional ventilation systems based solely on fixed thresholds. The project introduces a “predictive adaptive ventilation” paradigm, where decisions are driven not only by instantaneous sensor readings but by temporal, contextual, and multivariable data analysis.

At the edge AI level, VentiLabs implements an advanced sensor fusion strategy based on dominance and dynamic prioritization. Each gas sensor independently generates a ventilation demand, and the system computes the final PWM output using a dominant-maximum strategy, ensuring that the most hazardous contaminant immediately takes priority. To prevent oscillations, mechanical stress, and inefficient energy usage, temporal hysteresis mechanisms and stability windows are incorporated. Ventilation levels are only reduced if gas concentrations remain below safe thresholds for a defined period, introducing a simplified temporal derivative control that allows the system to anticipate rising or dissipating gas trends.

At the artificial intelligence layer, VentiLabs leverages models trained with MindSpore for anomaly detection and event prediction. These models are developed and trained in Huawei ModelArts using multivariate historical datasets that include gas concentrations, temperature, humidity, and system state variables. The model architecture is optimized for time-series analysis, enabling the detection of complex patterns that are typically invisible to rule-based systems, such as slow but persistent gas accumulation or dangerous combinations of moderately elevated pollutants.

A key innovation is the functional separation between cloud-based and edge-based inference. High-complexity models are trained, optimized, and evaluated in Huawei Cloud using Ascend AI resources, while compressed and quantified versions of the models are deployed locally on the physical system for real-time inference. This Edge–Cloud co-design significantly reduces latency, lowers energy consumption, and ensures autonomous operation even in scenarios with limited or unstable connectivity.
Furthermore, VentiLabs incorporates incremental learning capabilities. Data collected from multiple deployed devices can be aggregated to periodically retrain the models, allowing the system to continuously improve its accuracy and adaptability to new environments, pollutant profiles, or operational patterns. This evolutionary capability clearly differentiates VentiLabs from existing solutions, which are typically static and non-adaptive.
Overall, the algorithmic innovation of VentiLabs does not merely optimize ventilation control; it transforms a traditionally passive system into an intelligent, predictive, and self-improving infrastructure, fully aligned with the principles of modern industrial artificial intelligence and Huawei’s AI ecosystem.
